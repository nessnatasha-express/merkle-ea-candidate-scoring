Why This Framework Exists

What makes a great Enterprise Architect here

An EA at Adobe Integrated Services has to move fluidly between deep Adobe platform knowledge and the judgment to lead a client through ambiguity, a boardroom, and a delivery pod all in the same week. No single person walks in with all of it. This page breaks down the competencies we score against, why each one earns its weight, and what "good enough to hire" actually looks like versus "fully grown."

Industry Expertise

Not scored, because depth in one industry doesn't make someone a better or worse architect — it just means they've solved these problems in this vertical before. We track it as a staffing filter, not a grade.

Nobody Needs to Be a 100 Today

The long-term goal is a team where everyone eventually covers the full spread of competencies above. That's a multi-year bar, not a hiring bar. When we're bringing new EAs on board today, we're looking for candidates who land in the 75–85% range against the ideal benchmark — strong enough to be credible and productive from day one, with clear, specific gaps we can close through pairing, certification pushes, or bootcamps once they're in seat. A candidate scoring below that isn't disqualified outright, but it does mean a longer runway before they can carry an engagement solo. A candidate scoring near 100 on paper is worth a second look too — verify the depth is real before assuming it de-risks the hire.
