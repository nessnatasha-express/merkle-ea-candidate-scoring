DNA of an EA

What makes a great Enterprise Architect here

As an Enterprise Architect at Merkle, working within the Adobe Integrated Services group, you have to move fluidly between deep Adobe platform knowledge and the judgment to lead a client through ambiguity, a boardroom, and a delivery pod, all in the same week. No single person walks in with all of it. This page breaks down the competencies we score against, why each one earns its weight, and what "good enough to hire" actually looks like versus "fully grown."

How the 100 Points Break Down

Platform Prowess

Hands-on depth across the specific Adobe tools clients are actually paying us to run. This carries the most weight because credibility in the room depends on being able to go under the hood, not just gesture at the roadmap.

30%

Systems Intelligence

The connective tissue between tools — content, data, integrations, governance, modernization, and AI. This is what turns a pile of platform licenses into an actual working ecosystem for a client.

30%

Business Acumen

The human and organizational skill that makes the technical work land: leading executives, holding a room through ambiguity, and orchestrating a pod without formal authority.

tag only

Industry Expertise

Not scored, because depth in one industry doesn't make someone a better or worse architect — it just means they've solved these problems in this vertical before. We track it as a staffing filter, not a grade.

Nobody Needs to Be a 100 Today

The long-term goal is a team where everyone eventually covers the full spread of competencies above. That's a multi-year bar, not a hiring bar. When we're bringing new EAs on board today, we're looking for candidates who land in the 70–80% range against the ideal benchmark — strong enough to be credible and productive from day one, with clear, specific gaps we can close through pairing, certification pushes, or bootcamps once they're in seat. A candidate scoring below that isn't disqualified outright, but it does mean a longer runway before they can carry an engagement solo. A candidate scoring near 100 on paper is worth a second look too — verify the depth is real before assuming it de-risks the hire.

What to do with a score: anyone scoring above 50% can be sent to Natasha for a resume review and screen. Anyone scoring above 75% can go ahead and be scheduled directly for an interview with Natasha.
